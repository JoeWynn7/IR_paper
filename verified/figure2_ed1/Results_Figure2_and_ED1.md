# Results guide: Figure 2 and Extended Data Figure 1

## 1. Research question and scope

These figures examine whether directional inertia makes private reputations informative about behavioral strategies. Figure 2 shows illustrative reputation matrices and a scan over the two inertia depths. Extended Data Figure 1 evaluates the depth choices with independent repetitions and examines their sensitivity to the recognition criteria.

Both figures concern reputation formation at **fixed strategy composition**. ALLC, ALLD, and DISC have equal population sizes throughout each simulation. These results describe recognition and agreement; they do not establish evolutionary cooperation levels, cooperative basins, or resistance to invading strategies.

The current outputs are generated by [Figure2.ipynb](Figure2.ipynb) and [Extended_Data_Figure1.ipynb](Extended_Data_Figure1.ipynb). The combined figures are [Figure 2](figures/figure2/Figure2_combined.pdf) and [Extended Data Figure 1](figures/extended_data_figure1/Extended_Data_Figure1_combined.pdf).

## 2. Model features needed to read the figures

ALLC always intends to cooperate, ALLD always defects, and DISC intends to cooperate with recipients it privately regards as good. DISC observers assess the donor's realized action using their own private image of the recipient. Implementation and assessment errors are both $e_1=e_2=0.02$.

Each observer maintains a private internal record for each target. Negative assessments advance a good record through $k_1$ warning states before it becomes bad. Positive assessments advance a bad record through $k_2$ recovery states before it becomes good. A positive assessment clears any warning and returns the record directly to good; a negative assessment clears any recovery state and returns it directly to bad.

Consequently, completing the loss-of-standing path requires $k_1+1$ consecutive negative assessments, and completing recovery requires $k_2+1$ consecutive positive assessments. These are consecutive assessments recorded by the same observer about the same target, rather than consecutive actions by that target.

With full readout, $\alpha=\beta=1$, warning states are read as good and recovery states as bad. The internal record starts in the good state. Observers are sampled from DISC independently of the donor and recipient; self-images are retained. One sweep contains $n_{\mathrm{DISC}}N$ interaction events.

| Analysis | Population | Repetition and measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Figure 2 matrices | $N=90$, 30 individuals per strategy | One final snapshot per condition, after 1,000 sweeps; seed 2027. |
| Figure 2 depth panels | $N=150$, 50 per strategy | One run per depth pair and norm; 1,000 sweeps, burn-in 800, sampling every five sweeps, giving 41 sampling times. Base seed 2027. |
| Extended Data Figure 1 | $N=150$, 50 per strategy | 30 independent runs per depth pair and norm. Most use the same 1,000/800/5 measurement schedule; selected slowly relaxing conditions use longer windows. Base seed 20260918. |

The two depth datasets are separate experiments. The 30 repetitions in Extended Data Figure 1 must not be attributed to Figure 2.

## 3. Quantities being measured

Let $g_I$ be the mean probability that a DISC observer reads a type-$I$ target as good. It averages over observers, targets of that strategy, and the measurement window. Reputation separation is

$$
\Delta g=\frac{g_{\mathrm{DISC}}+g_{\mathrm{ALLC}}}{2}-g_{\mathrm{ALLD}}.
$$

Larger positive values indicate that the two cooperative strategies are regarded more favorably than unconditional defectors. This is a contrast between mean reputations, **not a classification accuracy or a percentage of correctly identified individuals**. Because it averages DISC and ALLC, the individual $g_I$ values remain relevant.

For a given target, disagreement is the probability that two distinct DISC observers give different binary readouts. The estimator uses their individual readout probabilities and accounts for sampling distinct observers in a finite population. Let $d_I$ denote its average for targets of strategy $I$. The plotted mean disagreement is

$$
\bar d=\frac{d_{\mathrm{DISC}}+d_{\mathrm{ALLC}}+d_{\mathrm{ALLD}}}{3}.
$$

All these quantities are dimensionless. Low disagreement can describe either informative agreement or agreement on an uninformative reputation. It must therefore be interpreted together with separation and the target-specific reputations.

## 4. Figure 2: private reputation matrices and the depth landscape

### Matrix groups a-d

The four columns correspond to Scoring, Shunning, Stern Judging, and Simple Standing. Each contains three matrix panels:

1. **Without inertia:** $k_1=k_2=1$ with $\alpha=\beta=0$, yielding instantaneous binary updating.
2. **Full one-step inertia:** $k_1=k_2=1$ with $\alpha=\beta=1$.
3. **Full multi-step inertia:** $\alpha=\beta=1$, with the displayed norm-specific depth pair.

The multi-step pairs are SC $(2,0)$, SH $(3,0)$, SJ $(4,3)$, and SS $(1,2)$.

Each matrix panel contains three $30\times30$ target blocks, ordered DISC, ALLC, and ALLD. Rows represent DISC observers and columns represent targets. Blue means good and white means bad. These are individual end-of-run realizations, not averages of repeated matrices.

The selected multi-step snapshots visibly distinguish the cooperative target blocks from the ALLD block more clearly than the corresponding one-step examples. The one-step examples also show why the norms need separate interpretation: Shunning can produce nearly universal bad opinions, whereas Stern Judging retains extensive private disagreement.

The current release uses seed 2027 for all 12 snapshots. Its SJ multi-step snapshot is a reproducible realization that differs from the earlier manually assembled SJ snapshot; the interpretation here refers to the current notebook output.

### Depth panels e-h

The horizontal axis is tolerance depth $k_1$ and the vertical axis is strictness depth $k_2$, each ranging from 0 to 6. Each norm therefore has 49 conditions, for 196 conditions in total.

- Cell color shows $\Delta g$, with a common scale from -1 to 1: blue is positive, red is negative, and white is zero.
- White circles show $\bar d$. Circle area scales with the square of disagreement in the plotting function; the size legend supplies the reference values.
- A black outline marks $(0,0)$, and a red outline marks full one-step inertia $(1,1)$.
- A gold star marks the shortest pair satisfying the specified recognition criteria in that panel's dataset.

The $(0,0)$ grid reference uses full readout with no intermediate states. It implements the same instantaneous binary-updating rule as the first matrix row, which retains one-step internal states but uses $\alpha=\beta=0$.

The landscapes show that the allocation between the two directions matters. Increasing a common total depth does not uniquely determine recognition: configurations with different allocations can have different separation and disagreement. The displayed representative regions are tolerance-dominated for SC and SH, bidirectional for SJ, and recovery-dominated for SS.

Because each condition in this scan has one independent run, the panels provide a depth landscape without a replicate-based uncertainty estimate. Time averaging within a run is not a substitute for independent repetitions. The repeated analysis is provided separately in Extended Data Figure 1.

## 5. Extended Data Figure 1: selection and threshold sensitivity

### Panels a-d: selecting compact recognition architectures

Each point represents one of the 49 depth pairs for a norm. The horizontal axis is mean disagreement, the vertical axis is reputation separation, and color encodes total depth $K=k_1+k_2$. Insets show the broader distribution of candidate configurations. The shaded qualifying region uses

$$
\Delta g\geq0.8,\qquad \bar d\leq0.1.
$$

Among qualifying configurations that pass the drift criterion, selection first minimizes $K$, then favors greater separation, lower disagreement, and finally smaller $k_1$ and $k_2$ to resolve ties. The star identifies this selection.

The following values come from the **30-repetition Extended Data Figure 1 dataset**. Brackets contain pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals for the corresponding means.

| Norm | Selected $(k_1,k_2)$ | $K$ | Separation $\Delta g$ | Mean disagreement $\bar d$ |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| SC | $(2,0)$ | 2 | 0.91950 [0.91916, 0.91981] | 0.06436 [0.06412, 0.06462] |
| SH | $(3,0)$ | 3 | 0.90589 [0.90561, 0.90620] | 0.06937 [0.06915, 0.06956] |
| SJ | $(4,3)$ | 7 | 0.88064 [0.88014, 0.88118] | 0.08123 [0.08088, 0.08154] |
| SS | $(1,2)$ | 3 | 0.91740 [0.91709, 0.91771] | 0.05617 [0.05596, 0.05639] |

The intervals are supplied in the numerical tables; the current figure does not draw them as error bars.

All four selected pairs satisfy both criteria. Full one-step inertia does not satisfy both under any norm in this dataset. SS is close: its one-step separation is 0.81524, but disagreement is 0.10810, above the specified limit.

SH provides a particularly useful distinction between agreement and recognition. Full one-step inertia gives disagreement of approximately 0.00162, but separation is only approximately 0.000035. The three target classes all have mean good reputations near 0.0008. Observers agree largely because they regard almost everyone as bad. Under the selected $(3,0)$ configuration, separation rises to 0.90589 while disagreement is 0.06937. The operational aim is therefore informative agreement, rather than minimizing disagreement alone.

### Panels e-h: dependence on the operational criteria

The horizontal axis varies the maximum allowed disagreement over $0.05,0.075,0.10,0.125,0.15$. The vertical axis varies the minimum required separation over $0.70,0.75,0.80,0.85,0.90$. Each cell gives the selected pair, and its color gives $K$. Gold outlines mark the default thresholds; dark boundaries identify cells selecting the same pair as the default.

These panels display selection based on the mean estimates. The saved table also contains a more conservative rule requiring the lower pointwise interval for separation and upper pointwise interval for disagreement to meet the criteria. At the default thresholds, both rules select the same four pairs.

The exact integers depend on the criteria. For example, requiring separation of at least 0.90 and disagreement of at most 0.05 selects SC $(3,1)$, SH $(4,1)$, SJ $(5,5)$, and SS $(1,3)$. The displayed sensitivity therefore supports a conditional interpretation of the representative pairs: they are minimum-depth selections within this grid and setting, rather than universal optimal architectures.

### Replication and drift checks

The raw table contains 5,880 records: four norms, 49 depth pairs, and 30 independent repetitions. Uncertainty is calculated with 2,000 bootstrap resamples of replicate means, using seed 2026091801. The intervals are pointwise, rather than simultaneous confidence bands across the grid.

For each architecture, the bootstrap intervals for separation drift and disagreement drift between the two halves of the measurement window must both lie strictly inside $(-0.01,0.01)$. All 196 architectures pass this saved-data criterion. Most runs have 41 measurement times. SJ $(4,5)$ uses 3,000 sweeps with burn-in 2,400, giving 121 measurements; SJ $(3,5)$ uses 50,000 sweeps with burn-in 40,000, giving 2,001 measurements. These are operational checks on the measured observables, rather than a proof of an infinite-time stationary distribution.

## 6. Brief mechanistic interpretation

The selected allocations are consistent with the different assessment signals generated by each norm:

| Norm | Prescribed assessment feature, before errors | Interpretation of the selected allocation |
|---|---|---|
| SC | Cooperation receives approval, but defection is condemned even when DISC refuses a recipient it regards as bad. | A longer warning path buffers negative assessments of conditional cooperators; $(2,0)$ combines this protection with immediate recovery after a positive assessment. |
| SH | Only cooperation with a recipient regarded as good receives approval. | Cooperative strategies can receive many negative assessments in a bad-reputation environment. The selected $(3,0)$ protects established good standing while allowing rapid recovery. |
| SJ | Cooperation with good recipients and defection against bad recipients receive approval. Disagreement about the recipient reverses the prescribed assessment of either action. | The selected $(4,3)$ combines protection against loss of good standing with a longer recovery requirement. This is consistent with addressing both negative signals about cooperative strategies and positive signals about defectors. |
| SS | Cooperation receives approval, and defection against a bad recipient also receives approval. | ALLD can receive positive assessments as well. The selected $(1,2)$ retains some protection of good standing while requiring more evidence for recovery. |

These explanations connect the observations to the update rules. The figures do not isolate every causal pathway or determine the particular integers from the norm alone. Increasing either depth changes the assessment environment through private reputations, and can affect cooperators and defectors simultaneously.

## 7. What the two figures jointly support

Figure 2 gives an intuitive view of private opinions and shows how recognition varies across directional depths. Extended Data Figure 1 supplies independent repetitions, an explicit selection rule, and a discrete sensitivity analysis for the representative configurations.

Together, they support the following restricted conclusion: **under the specified errors, equal strategy composition, all-good initialization, and direct-reset rule, norm-dependent allocations of directional inertia can combine stronger reputational separation with low private disagreement; full one-step inertia does not meet both selected recognition criteria.**

The figures do not show that deeper memory is always better, that the selected pairs are optimal outside the tested grid, or that improved recognition guarantees cooperative evolutionary stability. Differences should not be called statistically significant without a corresponding statistical test. Evolutionary conclusions require the separate payoff and strategy-dynamics analyses.
