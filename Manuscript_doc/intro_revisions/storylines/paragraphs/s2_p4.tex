Here, we develop a model of indirect reciprocity in which each observer integrates its own observations over time, without sharing information and without observing more than in standard private-assessment models (Fig.~1). Every observer keeps a private record of each individual. Negative assessments move an individual in good standing through a sequence of warning states before the observer's verdict turns bad, and positive assessments move an individual in bad standing through recovery states before it turns good. The two paths can differ in length, so that an observer may require more evidence to condemn than to rehabilitate, or the reverse. Each intermediate state is interpreted as good or bad with a probability that sets how strongly incomplete evidence already affects the observer's behaviour.
