## 2. Roles of the leading eight, private reputations and the punishment dilemma

The Introduction now builds the argument in this order.

1. **Leading eight.** They are the best-known answer to how goodness should be defined. Their assumptions are now stated: binary and public reputations, so that all individuals hold the same reputation of each other and an assessment error affects every observer alike.
2. **Private reputations.** Each observer keeps its own record. Different interactions and independent errors produce different reputations of the same individual.
3. **Punishment dilemma.** Under Stern Judging and Simple Standing, a justified refusal should receive a positive assessment. An observer that disagrees with the donor about the recipient assigns a negative one instead (Okada 2020; Michel-Mata et al. 2024).
4. **Failure results.** Each is stated with its condition (see section 5).
5. **Proposed solutions.** They reduce disagreement or the negative assessments it causes. None of them changes what a negative assessment, once assigned, does to the donor's reputation. This replaces the weak ending "and they leave the updating of reputations unchanged".
6. **Example 1, paragraph 3.** It picks up the misjudged refusal and states who acts (the discriminator), who assesses (the observer) and who updates the record (the observer). It then contrasts instantaneous updating, under which one misjudgment produces further misjudgments, with a warning state that absorbs it.
7. **Example 2, paragraph 4.** It covers the defector excused under Stern Judging and Simple Standing. A warning path protects the defector's undeserved good standing. A recovery path removes that protection but delays the return of a wrongly condemned cooperator. The two examples together motivate asymmetric reputational inertia.

## 5. Claims matched to the evidence

**Prior work**
- Hilbe et al. 2018: most leading-eight norms fail to evolve under private and noisy assessment.
- Perret et al. 2021: the evolutionarily stable rules that exist without errors disappear once errors occur.
- Okada et al. 2018: with a single observer per interaction, Stern Judging and Shunning have no stable cooperative equilibrium.
- Fujimoto & Ohtsuki 2023: Stern Judging cannot be evolutionarily stable even when every individual observes every interaction, whereas Simple Standing can. This condition is now stated in the Discussion as well as in the Results.
- Murase & Hilbe 2024: no cooperative norm is evolutionarily stable when the reputations held by different observers are statistically independent.
- Generosity (Schmid et al. 2021, Sci. Rep.): generous *assessment* lets defectors invade, and generous *action* helps only in a few cases.
  - The earlier phrase "make assessment more lenient through … generosity" overstated their result.
  - The Introduction now says "responding generously to some defections …, although generous assessment can let defectors invade".
- Murase & Hilbe 2023 (stochastic and dual updates) analyse the public-information regime. They are now cited only as prior work on updating, not for private assessment.
- Michel-Mata et al. 2024: the condition "provided that observers tolerate some negative ones" is kept.
- Clark et al. 2020: the condition "social dilemmas with strategic complementarity" was added.
- Prior work on updating is acknowledged explicitly:
  - quantitative scores, samples of observations and records of defections;
  - intermediate, ternary and continuous reputations, and gradual change;
  - stochastic updates and updates of the recipient's reputation;
  - no update of the donor's reputation when the recipient is in bad standing.

**This paper's own findings, as stated in the Introduction**
- "observers in simulated populations" (the reputational results come from simulations);
- "in the evolutionary dynamics of unconditional cooperators, unconditional defectors and discriminators";
- "when each interaction has a single observer";
- "in the limit of rare errors" for the requirement on every rule that turns assessments into reputations (Supplementary Note 6);
- "The success of such norms therefore depends on agreement about recipients" replaces "sustain cooperation only if observers agree", which conflicted with Fujimoto & Ohtsuki 2023.

**Discussion**
- "In our simulations" for the agreement between observers.
- "Nor does independence prevent discriminators from resisting both unconditional strategies" replaces "prevent stable cooperation". The paper itself shows that a DISC population cannot strictly resist deviations in both directions.
- "In our model" for the Stern Judging result.
- "In the limit of rare errors" for the general resolution of the tolerance problem.

## 6. Items for author verification

Michel-Mata et al. 2024 attribute the terms "scoring dilemma" and "punishment dilemma" to Berger & Grune (2016), *On the stability of cooperation under indirect reciprocity with first-order information*, Games Econ. Behav. 98, 19–33. Consider adding that paper.

   The paper was checked only through search summaries in an earlier round, so all three uses need confirming.
